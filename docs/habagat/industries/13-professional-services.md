# Document 13 — Professional Services (Legal, Consulting, Accounting, Engineering)

> 13 agent use cases. Notation per [Document 00](../00-research-method-and-taxonomy.md).

## Executive take
- Professional services sell **leveraged expert time**. Agents attack the leverage model directly: they do the associate-level work, which is simultaneously the profession's biggest cost and its biggest margin.
- This creates the sector's defining tension: **the billable hour punishes efficiency**. Habagat's commercial argument must be about capacity, win rate and fixed-fee margin — not about reducing hours billed. Firms moving to fixed and outcome pricing are the natural early adopters.
- Highest-value entry points: **PS-02 Document Review & Due Diligence**, **PS-06 Audit Testing & Evidence**, **PS-09 Proposal & Pitch Production**. The first two are pure cost; the third is pure revenue.
- Confidentiality is existential here. Client matter separation, conflict management and privilege protection must be architectural — a leak across a Chinese wall is a firm-ending event.

### PS-01 — Legal & Technical Research
**A3 · L1 · B2 · Idea-to-market**
**Pain** — Research is the associate's core task and the client's least favourite line item. It is slow, variable in quality, and frequently repeats work the firm has already done.
**Trigger** — Research question raised on a matter or engagement.
**Workflow** — 1) Clarify the question: the jurisdiction, the legal or technical issue, the client's factual position, and the purpose of the advice. 2) **Search the firm's own prior work first** — the single most under-used asset in every professional firm, and the one with the highest quality bar because it has already been reviewed. 3) Search authoritative external sources: legislation, case law, regulatory guidance, technical standards, and commentary. 4) Verify currency: is the authority still good law, is the standard still current, has it been superseded, distinguished or overruled? Citing overruled authority is a professional negligence exposure. 5) Synthesise the position, distinguishing settled law from contested, and identifying the arguments on both sides. 6) Draft the research note with **complete and verified citations** — every citation checked to exist and say what it is cited for, since fabricated authority has already ended careers. 7) Identify the gaps and the questions requiring senior judgement. 8) File the note into the knowledge base so the next matter benefits.
**Systems** — Legal research platforms (Westlaw/LexisNexis/Practical Law), internal document management, precedent bank, standards libraries.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Research turnaround; reuse of internal precedent; citation accuracy (must be 100%); partner review effort; write-offs on research time.
**Human gate** — **A qualified professional owns all advice.** The agent produces research, never advice, and never communicates with clients.

### PS-02 — Document Review & Due Diligence
**A2 · L2 · B3 · Risk-to-assurance**
**Pain** — Transactional and litigation document review is the profession's largest labour pool: thousands of documents reviewed for issues, at enormous cost, with quality declining as reviewers tire.
**Trigger** — Data room opened, disclosure set received, or a review scope defined.
**Workflow** — 1) Define the review protocol: what issues matter, what the client's risk thresholds are, and what categorisation is required. 2) Classify and organise the corpus: document types, parties, dates, and relationships between documents. 3) **Extract the issues that matter for this deal or dispute**: change-of-control, assignment restrictions, exclusivity, termination rights, liability caps, IP ownership, data protection, and unusual terms. 4) Assess materiality against the client's stated thresholds rather than flagging everything — a report flagging 4,000 items is as useless as one flagging none. 5) Identify what is missing: contracts referenced but not provided, unsigned agreements, and gaps in a chain. Missing documents are frequently the real finding. 6) Draft the due-diligence report with findings, risk ratings, and recommended actions or deal-protection mechanisms. 7) For litigation, support privilege review and responsiveness assessment with the reasoning, and flag borderline privilege calls for a lawyer. 8) Maintain a complete audit trail of the review, which may itself be scrutinised.
**Systems** — Document review platform (Relativity/Everlaw), data rooms, document intelligence, matter management.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Documents reviewed per hour; issue-detection recall against QC sampling; cost per document; report cycle time; issues missed and found later (the metric that matters).
**Human gate** — **Privilege determinations and all findings communicated to clients are lawyer-reviewed.** Materiality thresholds are set by the engagement partner.

### PS-03 — Contract Drafting & Negotiation Support
**A2 · L2 · B3 · Order-to-cash**
**Pain** — Drafting from precedent is manual and error-prone: stale clauses, inconsistent definitions, and cross-references that break when clauses move.
**Trigger** — Drafting instruction, or a counterparty markup received.
**Workflow** — 1) Select the appropriate precedent from the firm's bank based on transaction type, jurisdiction, and the client's position. 2) Draft using the deal parameters, ensuring internal consistency of defined terms, cross-references, schedules and numbering — the mechanical errors that consume review time. 3) Apply the client's known preferences and prior positions from previous matters. 4) On receiving a markup, produce a **precise change analysis**: what changed, what it means commercially and legally, and how significant it is. Counterparties bury material changes in formatting churn. 5) Assess each change against the client's playbook and recommend accept, counter, or escalate, with the counter language drafted. 6) Track the negotiation history across versions so nobody re-concedes a point already won. 7) Run a completeness and consistency check before execution. 8) On execution, extract obligations into the client's register (see UC-X03).
**Systems** — Document management, precedent bank, comparison tools, CLM, negotiation history.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Drafting time; markup analysis turnaround; consistency defects found pre-execution; negotiation cycles; positions conceded that the playbook says should not be.
**Human gate** — **All drafting is reviewed and all advice given by a qualified lawyer.** The agent never negotiates with a counterparty.

### PS-04 — Matter & Engagement Management
**A5 · L3 · B3 · Plan-to-produce**
**Pain** — Matters run over budget silently; scope creep is discovered at billing; utilisation and profitability are known only in arrears.
**Trigger** — Matter opened, milestone reached, or a budget threshold crossed.
**Workflow** — 1) On opening, run the intake checks: conflicts, AML/KYC on the client, sanctions screening, and engagement terms. 2) Build the matter plan: phases, tasks, resourcing, budget, and deadlines including any statutory or court-imposed dates. 3) Monitor progress and cost against plan continuously, and **forecast the outturn rather than reporting the spend** — knowing you will overrun in three weeks is actionable; knowing you overran is not. 4) Detect scope creep by comparing work performed against the engagement scope, and prompt the variation conversation early while the client is still receptive. 5) Manage deadlines with escalation — missed limitation dates and court deadlines are the profession's most serious risk. 6) Support resourcing: skills match, availability, and development opportunities for juniors. 7) Prepare matter status reports for the client with progress, spend and next steps. 8) Analyse realisation and profitability by matter type to inform pricing.
**Systems** — Practice management, time recording, billing, conflicts and AML systems, project management, calendar.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Budget variance; realisation rate; write-offs; deadline compliance (must be 100%); scope variations agreed vs absorbed; matter profitability.
**Human gate** — Conflict clearance and client acceptance are partner decisions; fee variations are commercially agreed.

### PS-05 — Conflicts, Client Acceptance & Risk Screening
**A8 · L2 · B4 · Risk-to-assurance**
**Pain** — Conflict checking across a large firm's client and matter history is complex; missing a conflict is a professional and regulatory breach and can require the firm to withdraw mid-matter.
**Trigger** — New client or matter enquiry.
**Workflow** — 1) Identify all relevant parties: client, counterparties, related entities, group companies, ultimate owners, and key individuals. 2) **Resolve corporate structures and name variants** — conflicts hide in subsidiaries and renamed entities, which is exactly where name-matching fails. 3) Search the firm's client and matter database for direct, positional and commercial conflicts. 4) Assess each hit: is it an actual conflict, a potential conflict requiring informed consent, or a false positive? Provide the reasoning for the risk partner. 5) Run AML/KYC and sanctions screening on the client and beneficial owners, with source-of-funds assessment where required. 6) Assess client and matter risk: sector, jurisdiction, PEP exposure, reputational factors, and the firm's own risk appetite. 7) Prepare the acceptance pack with recommended conditions: information barriers, engagement letter terms, or declining. 8) On acceptance, implement and monitor information barriers technically.
**Systems** — Conflicts database, client/matter records, corporate registries, sanctions and PEP screening, document management access controls.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Conflict check turnaround; conflicts missed (must be zero); false-positive reduction; AML compliance; barriers implemented correctly.
**Human gate** — **Risk partners make all acceptance and conflict decisions.** Ethical walls are enforced technically at the identity and document layer, not by policy alone.

### PS-06 — Audit Testing & Evidence Gathering
**A8 · L2 · B4 · Risk-to-assurance**
**Pain** — Audit is sample-based because full-population testing was historically impossible. Juniors spend their time on vouching and tie-outs; audit quality findings from regulators keep rising.
**Trigger** — Audit planning, fieldwork phase, or a continuous-audit cycle.
**Workflow** — 1) Support risk assessment: analyse the client's financial data for unusual patterns, ratio movements and account-level anomalies, and identify where risk of material misstatement is concentrated. 2) **Test full populations rather than samples** where the data supports it — journal entries, revenue transactions, expenses — which changes the nature of audit evidence rather than merely speeding it up. 3) Perform substantive procedures: three-way matching, cut-off testing, reconciliations, and recalculation. 4) Vouch transactions to supporting documentation by retrieving and matching the evidence. 5) Test controls by examining the full population of control operations rather than 25 samples. 6) Identify exceptions, investigate them, and distinguish errors from misstatements from control failures. 7) Draft the working papers to the firm's methodology with the evidence linked, so review is possible and the file survives inspection. 8) Draft management letter points with the finding, the risk and the recommendation.
**Systems** — Audit platform (CaseWare/Inflo/proprietary), client ERP data, document repositories, analytics tools.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Population coverage vs sampling; hours per audit; exceptions identified; **file review and regulatory inspection outcomes**; audit adjustments proposed.
**Human gate** — **All audit judgements, conclusions and opinions are made by qualified auditors.** Independence rules constrain what the audit firm may do for the client; the agent's scope is bounded by them.

### PS-07 — Tax Compliance & Advisory Support
**A2 · L2 · B4 · Record-to-report**
**Pain** — Tax compliance is high-volume, deadline-driven and detail-intensive; advisory work requires synthesising complex, frequently-changing rules across jurisdictions.
**Trigger** — Compliance deadline, client data received, or an advisory question.
**Workflow** — 1) Gather and validate the client's data against the requirements for the return, chasing specific gaps rather than sending a generic checklist. 2) Compute the tax position: adjustments, reliefs, allowances, losses, and group relief, applying the current rules for the period. 3) **Identify claims and elections the client is entitled to and has not made** — the value-add that clients notice, and that time pressure squeezes out. 4) Perform reasonableness checks against prior years and against the underlying business, and explain material movements. 5) Identify uncertain tax positions and matters requiring disclosure. 6) Prepare the return and the supporting computation with the workings visible. 7) For advisory questions, research the position across the relevant jurisdictions and identify the interactions — cross-border interaction is where advice goes wrong. 8) Track legislative change and assess the impact on the client base, generating proactive advisory opportunities.
**Systems** — Tax compliance software, client accounting data, tax research platforms, practice management.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Returns per preparer; on-time filing (100%); review points per return; reliefs identified; enquiry rate; advisory opportunities generated.
**Human gate** — **All returns are reviewed and signed by a qualified tax professional.** Advice is professional judgement; disclosure decisions carry personal liability.

### PS-08 — Consulting Analysis & Deliverable Production
**A3 · L1 · B2 · Idea-to-market**
**Pain** — Consulting deliverables are built from scratch each time: market analysis, benchmarking, data analysis and slide production consume junior time and compress the thinking.
**Trigger** — Engagement workstream, or an analysis request.
**Workflow** — 1) Structure the problem: decompose the client question into a hypothesis tree with the analyses needed to test each branch. 2) Assemble data from client systems, the firm's benchmark databases, and external sources, with quality assessment. 3) Perform the analysis, stating assumptions explicitly and showing sensitivity to them — an unsensitised model is a false precision machine. 4) **Search the firm's prior engagements** for relevant experience, methods and benchmarks, respecting client confidentiality boundaries absolutely. 5) Synthesise findings into the answer, distinguishing what the data supports from what it suggests from what the team believes. 6) Draft the deliverable in the firm's structure with a genuine storyline rather than a data dump. 7) Identify the weakest links in the argument, so the team can strengthen them before the client does. 8) Support the client-review cycle with change tracking.
**Systems** — Knowledge management, benchmark databases, data analysis environment, presentation tooling, engagement repositories.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Analysis cycle time; reuse of firm knowledge; deliverable revision cycles; consultant time on thinking vs production; client feedback.
**Human gate** — **Client confidentiality boundaries are enforced technically**; engagement partners own all client-facing content and recommendations.

### PS-09 — Proposal, Pitch & Panel Response Production
**A3 · L1 · B2 · Demand creation**
**Pain** — Proposals are produced under deadline by the same senior people who are delivering work; credentials and CVs are re-written per pitch; win rates are unmeasured.
**Trigger** — RFP received, pitch opportunity, or a panel review.
**Workflow** — 1) Analyse the RFP: requirements, evaluation criteria, weightings, mandatory elements, and constraints. 2) Support the bid/no-bid decision with the relevant history: prior work with this client, win rate in this segment, capability fit and capacity. 3) Assemble credentials matched specifically to the client's issues, with permission status verified for every named client reference. 4) Generate tailored CVs for the proposed team emphasising the relevant experience. 5) Draft the technical response against each criterion, drawing on the firm's approved content and prior winning bids. 6) Support pricing with historical delivery cost data for comparable work — the analysis that prevents both underpricing and losing on price. 7) Check compliance against every mandatory requirement and produce the compliance matrix. 8) After the outcome, capture win/loss reasons and update the content library.
**Systems** — CRM, proposal content library, credentials database, financial systems, resource management.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Proposal cycle time; partner hours per pitch; win rate; content reuse; pricing accuracy vs delivered cost.
**Human gate** — Partners own pricing, commitments and all client-facing content; client references require documented consent.

### PS-10 — Knowledge Management & Precedent Curation
**A6 · L2 · B2 · Idea-to-market**
**Pain** — Professional firms generate enormous knowledge and capture almost none of it. Knowledge systems fail because contribution is manual and the incentive is absent.
**Trigger** — Matter closed, document created, or a knowledge query.
**Workflow** — 1) Identify knowledge-worthy outputs automatically from matter activity: novel arguments, high-quality precedents, well-structured advice notes, and successful positions. 2) **Anonymise and generalise** the document into a reusable precedent, removing client-identifying content — the step that makes capture safe and that nobody does manually. 3) Classify against the firm's taxonomy with rich metadata: jurisdiction, transaction type, party position, and issues addressed. 4) Assess quality and currency, and flag precedents superseded by legal or regulatory change so nobody uses stale material. 5) Maintain the knowledge base: deduplicate, retire, and identify gaps where the firm has no precedent for something it does frequently. 6) Serve knowledge at the point of need inside the drafting and research workflows rather than in a portal nobody visits. 7) Capture expertise location: who in the firm has actually done this before.
**Systems** — Document management, knowledge platform, matter management, taxonomy, HR/expertise data.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Precedent reuse rate; time to find relevant prior work; knowledge-base currency; contribution without manual effort; drafting time reduction.
**Human gate** — Knowledge lawyers approve precedents for publication; confidentiality and anonymisation are verified before release.

### PS-11 — Time, Billing & Revenue Realisation
**A6 · L3 · B3 · Order-to-cash**
**Pain** — Time recording is late and incomplete, which loses revenue directly; bill preparation and narrative writing consume partner time; client billing disputes delay cash.
**Trigger** — Work performed, billing cycle, or a client query.
**Workflow** — 1) Prompt time capture from actual activity signals — documents worked on, emails, calendar, calls — as a **draft for the professional to confirm**, never as an automatic entry, since the record must be their honest attestation. 2) Improve narratives: convert terse entries into client-appropriate descriptions that comply with billing guidelines. 3) Check compliance with client billing guidelines before billing, which is where most write-offs originate — outside counsel guidelines reject specific task codes, staffing levels and activities. 4) Assemble the draft bill with time, disbursements and the narrative, and flag entries likely to be challenged. 5) Support the partner's write-off and write-up decisions with the matter context and realisation history. 6) Handle client billing queries by retrieving the underlying detail and explaining the work. 7) Manage collections with relationship awareness (see UC-X13). 8) Analyse realisation leakage by cause: unrecorded time, guideline breaches, write-offs, and discounts.
**Systems** — Time recording, practice management, billing, e-billing platforms, client guidelines repository.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Time recorded within 24 hours; realisation rate; e-billing rejection rate; lock-up days; billing query volume; leakage by cause.
**Human gate** — **Timekeepers confirm their own time entries** — this is an ethical requirement, not a workflow preference. Partners approve all bills and write-offs.

### PS-12 — Regulatory & Professional Compliance
**A8 · L2 · B4 · Risk-to-assurance**
**Pain** — Firms face professional regulation (conduct rules, CPD, independence, client money) plus general regulation (AML, data protection). Breaches carry personal and firm-level consequences.
**Trigger** — Compliance cycle, regulatory change, or a compliance event.
**Workflow** — 1) Maintain the obligations register mapped to owners, evidence and frequency. 2) Monitor independence: for audit firms, track prohibited services, financial interests and relationships against every audit client — a continuous, high-stakes, data-intensive check. 3) Monitor client money handling against the rules, with breach detection and reporting obligations. 4) Track professional obligations: practising certificates, CPD, insurance, and supervision arrangements. 5) Manage AML compliance: client due diligence currency, risk assessments, and the firm-wide risk assessment. 6) Detect conduct risks: undisclosed conflicts, confidentiality breaches, and information-barrier failures. 7) Prepare regulatory returns and inspection evidence. 8) Manage complaints and claims with root-cause analysis feeding risk management.
**Systems** — Practice management, client money systems, HR/CPD records, AML systems, conflicts, GRC.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Compliance breaches; independence violations (must be zero); AML file quality; CPD compliance; regulatory inspection outcomes; claims frequency.
**Human gate** — The compliance officer and money-laundering reporting officer hold personal regulatory accountability; all reportable breaches are human-assessed and reported.

### PS-13 — Engineering & Technical Design Review
**A4 · L2 · B4 · Idea-to-market**
**Pain** — Engineering consultancies produce designs, calculations and reports requiring compliance with standards, codes and regulations; review is manual and errors carry safety and liability consequences.
**Trigger** — Design produced, review milestone, or a standards change.
**Workflow** — 1) Identify applicable codes, standards and regulations for the project type, jurisdiction and client requirements. 2) Check the design against the requirements systematically, item by item, with the source clause cited for each check. 3) Verify calculations: methodology, inputs, assumptions and results, and check that assumptions are stated and defensible. 4) **Check consistency across the deliverable set** — drawings, specifications, calculations and schedules must agree, and in practice they routinely do not. 5) Identify buildability, maintainability and safety-in-design issues, which are the risks that surface expensively on site. 6) Check interfaces with other disciplines and flag coordination clashes. 7) Draft the review report with findings by severity and the clause each relates to. 8) Track finding closure and verify resolution before issue. 9) Monitor standards changes and assess impact on live and completed projects.
**Systems** — CAD/BIM, calculation software, standards libraries, document management, design review tools.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Review cycle time; findings by severity; errors escaping to construction; rework cost; standards compliance; professional indemnity claims.
**Human gate** — **Chartered engineers sign all designs and take professional responsibility.** Safety-critical elements require independent review by a competent person; agent output is never a substitute.

## Professional-obligation notes for this vertical
| Topic | Impact on agent design |
|---|---|
| **Confidentiality & privilege** | Client matter separation must be technically enforced. Agents must not be able to surface one client's information in another's matter — this is the single most important architectural requirement in the vertical. |
| **Information barriers** | Ethical walls must be enforced at the identity and retrieval layer. A wall that exists in policy but not in the index is not a wall. |
| **Professional responsibility** | A qualified professional must own every piece of advice, every opinion and every signed document. Agents are never the source of professional judgement. |
| **Independence (audit)** | The services an audit firm may provide to an audit client are restricted; agent deployments must be checked against independence rules like any other service. |
| **Citation integrity** | Fabricated authority is a professional catastrophe. Every citation must be verified to exist and to support the proposition — this is a hard, automated check, not a review instruction. |
| **Billing ethics** | Time records must be the timekeeper's own attestation. Automated time entry without confirmation is an ethical breach. |
