# Document 14 — Real Estate & Construction

> 13 agent use cases. Notation per [Document 00](../00-research-method-and-taxonomy.md).

## Executive take
- Construction is the least digitised major industry and the one with the worst productivity record. That makes it both the largest opportunity and the hardest sale — **agents must meet the industry where it is: email, PDFs, drawings and phone calls.**
- The money is in **contract administration**: variations, claims, payment applications and delay analysis. These are document-and-entitlement problems, which is precisely the agent-fit profile, and they decide whether a project makes or loses money.
- In real estate, the highest-value agents are **RE-08 Lease Abstraction & Administration** and **RE-01 Tenant Service** — recurring, document-heavy, and directly tied to net operating income.
- Safety and building-regulation compliance (post-Grenfell in the UK, similar tightening elsewhere) has created a **golden-thread documentation obligation** that most contractors cannot meet manually. That is a compliance-driven buying trigger.

### RE-01 — Tenant & Occupier Service Management
**A1 · L3 · B3 · Issue-to-resolution**
**Pain** — Property managers handle high volumes of tenant requests across portfolios; response times drive tenant satisfaction and renewal, and maintenance requests are triaged inconsistently.
**Trigger** — Tenant contacts by app, email, phone, or portal.
**Workflow** — 1) Identify the tenant, unit and lease, and their entitlements under it. 2) Classify the request: maintenance, lease question, billing, complaint, or a service request. 3) For maintenance, triage severity — **emergency (safety, water ingress, heating loss, security), urgent, or routine** — using defined criteria rather than tenant language, since tenants describe everything as urgent and some genuinely are. 4) Determine responsibility: landlord or tenant obligation under the lease, which determines who pays and prevents the most common dispute. 5) Dispatch to the right contractor with the access arrangements, the diagnosis and the SLA. 6) For lease questions, answer from the actual lease terms with the clause cited. 7) For billing, decompose the service charge and explain it — service-charge disputes are the largest source of tenant friction. 8) Track resolution, verify completion, and follow up on satisfaction. 9) Escalate complaints, disrepair claims and safety issues immediately.
**Systems** — Property management system, lease repository, contractor management, tenant portal, accounting.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Response and resolution times by priority; first-time-fix; tenant satisfaction and retention; emergency response compliance; repeat requests.
**Human gate** — Disrepair claims, safety issues, lease disputes and any legal notice.

### RE-02 — Tender Preparation & Bid Management
**A3 · L2 · B3 · Demand creation**
**Pain** — Contractors bid on far more work than they win; estimating teams are the bottleneck; take-off and pricing are manual and error-prone, and a pricing error is carried for the life of the project.
**Trigger** — Tender invitation received.
**Workflow** — 1) Analyse the tender documents: scope, specification, drawings, contract terms, programme and qualification requirements. 2) Support the bid/no-bid decision: capability fit, capacity, geography, client history, competition, and — critically — the contract's risk profile. 3) Extract the scope into a structured take-off and bill of quantities where not provided. 4) **Identify scope gaps, ambiguities and risk-transfer clauses** — the things that turn a profitable bid into a loss-making project. Onerous terms are found by reading, and estimating teams have no time to read. 5) Price from the cost database, current supplier and subcontractor quotes, and productivity data from comparable completed projects. 6) Build the programme and assess buildability and resource feasibility. 7) Quantify risk and recommend contingency, with the specific risks named rather than a blanket percentage. 8) Assemble the submission including all qualification and quality requirements. 9) Track outcomes and feed actual cost back into the estimating database — the loop that almost no contractor closes.
**Systems** — Estimating software, cost databases, CAD/BIM, supplier quotes, project historical data, CRM.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Bids per estimator; win rate; **estimated vs actual cost variance**; risks identified pre-contract that materialised; qualification compliance.
**Human gate** — Commercial approval of price, risk and contract terms; all contractual qualifications are legally reviewed.

### RE-03 — Contract Administration & Variation Management
**A2 · L2 · B4 · Order-to-cash**
**Pain** — Variations, instructions and changes flow constantly; entitlement is lost through late notification, poor records and missed contractual deadlines. This is where project margin is destroyed.
**Trigger** — Instruction issued, change identified, or a contractual notice deadline approaching.
**Workflow** — 1) Identify events constituting a change: instructions, drawing revisions, specification changes, site conditions, and client-caused delay. 2) Determine the contractual mechanism and, above all, the **notification deadline — most entitlement is lost by failing to notify in time**, and the agent's highest-value function is the clock. 3) Draft the required notice with the contractual references and the necessary particulars. 4) Assess the entitlement: cost, time, and the evidence required to substantiate each. 5) Assemble the substantiation: records, correspondence, site diaries, labour and plant allocation, and photographs. 6) Value the variation per the contract's valuation rules. 7) Track the change through the approval process and into the payment application. 8) Maintain the change register with status, value and exposure, so the commercial position is known in real time rather than at final account. 9) Flag where the accumulated changes have materially altered the project and warrant a formal position.
**Systems** — Contract management, project management, cost management, document control, site records.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Notices served within contractual deadlines (100% required); variations recovered vs incurred; change register accuracy; final account settlement vs claimed; disputes avoided.
**Human gate** — Commercial managers approve all contractual notices and claims; legal review for significant claims and any dispute.

### RE-04 — Programme, Progress & Delay Analysis
**A6 · L2 · B4 · Plan-to-produce**
**Pain** — Programmes are updated late and optimistically; delay is recognised when it is already critical; and delay claims fail because the records do not support the analysis.
**Trigger** — Progress reporting cycle, delay event, or a programme update.
**Workflow** — 1) Capture actual progress against the programme from site records, and check its plausibility rather than accepting reported percentages, which are systematically optimistic. 2) Update the schedule and recompute the critical path and float, identifying the activities that now drive completion. 3) Detect emerging delay early: activities trending behind, resources not mobilised, approvals and information outstanding. 4) **Analyse causation for delay events** — which activity, whose responsibility, and what the effect on completion is — using a recognised method (time impact analysis or as-planned versus as-built). 5) Assemble the contemporaneous records that substantiate the analysis; delay claims are won and lost on records, not arguments. 6) Model recovery options and their cost: acceleration, resequencing, additional resource, and their realistic effect. 7) Generate the progress report with the honest position, the risks and the required decisions. 8) Track outstanding information and approvals with the responsible party and the impact of each.
**Systems** — Planning software (P6/MSP), site progress capture, document control, cost systems, BIM.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Programme accuracy; delay identified before it hits the critical path; extension-of-time success rate; acceleration cost; information-request turnaround.
**Human gate** — Programme submissions and delay claims are commercially and legally approved; planners own the schedule logic.

### RE-05 — Site Quality, Snagging & Handover
**A8 · L3 · B4 · Risk-to-assurance**
**Pain** — Defects found at handover are expensive to fix and delay completion; quality records are paper-based; and the golden-thread of building-safety information is frequently incomplete.
**Trigger** — Inspection performed, work package complete, or a handover milestone.
**Workflow** — 1) Structure inspection requirements from the specification, the inspection and test plan, and regulatory requirements. 2) Capture inspection results with photographic evidence, location referenced to the model or drawing. 3) Raise defects with location, description, responsibility, severity and required rectification — vague snag lists cause rework and disputes. 4) **Track rectification to verified closure**, not to a contractor's assertion of completion. 5) Detect quality patterns by trade, subcontractor, location and work type — recurring defects indicate a process or competence problem worth fixing once. 6) Assemble the handover documentation set: as-built information, certificates, test results, warranties, O&M manuals, and the building-safety information required by regulation. 7) Verify completeness against the requirements before handover rather than discovering the gaps afterwards, when the subcontractors have gone. 8) Manage the defects-liability period with the same rigour.
**Systems** — Site quality apps, BIM/CDE, document management, subcontractor systems, asset information requirements.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Defects at handover; rectification cycle time; handover documentation completeness; defects-liability-period cost; recurring defect elimination.
**Human gate** — **Statutory sign-offs are made by competent persons**; building-safety and fire-safety matters follow regulatory process with named accountable persons.

### RE-06 — Health & Safety Management on Site
**A8 · L2 · B4 · Risk-to-assurance**
**Pain** — Construction has among the highest fatality rates of any industry. Safety documentation (RAMS, permits, inductions, competence) is voluminous and its quality is variable.
**Trigger** — Work planned, permit requested, incident or observation reported, or an inspection.
**Workflow** — 1) Verify competence and authorisation before work: cards, certifications, inductions, and training currency for the specific task. 2) Review risk assessments and method statements against the actual task and site conditions, and identify inadequate or generic RAMS — the copy-paste RAMS is a known and lethal failure mode. 3) Manage permits to work: high-risk activities, isolations, hot work, confined space, and lifting, with the required checks and authorisations. 4) Detect activity conflicts — concurrent operations that create risk together even though each is safe alone, which is a leading cause of serious incidents. 5) Process observations, near-misses and incidents with structured capture and immediate escalation for anything serious. 6) **Analyse leading indicators**: observation rates, permit compliance, and the conditions preceding near-misses. 7) Track corrective actions to verified closure. 8) Maintain the safety record for regulatory inspection and, if the worst happens, for investigation.
**Systems** — Safety management platform, competence/card verification, permit systems, site access control, incident reporting.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Incident and near-miss rates; RAMS quality; permit compliance; competence verification coverage; corrective-action closure; regulatory findings.
**Human gate** — **All safety decisions are made by competent, appointed persons.** The agent may never authorise work; it verifies, prompts and records. Serious incidents follow statutory process.

### RE-07 — Subcontractor & Supply Chain Management
**A8 · L3 · B3 · Procure-to-pay**
**Pain** — Contractors depend on hundreds of subcontractors whose competence, capacity, financial stability and compliance determine project outcomes. Prequalification is periodic and paper-based.
**Trigger** — Package procurement, subcontractor onboarding, or a performance signal.
**Workflow** — 1) Maintain the subcontractor record: capability, capacity, accreditations, insurance, financial standing, and performance history. 2) Verify compliance evidence continuously rather than annually: insurance currency, accreditations, right-to-work, and — where applicable — modern-slavery and labour-standards evidence. 3) **Monitor financial distress signals**, because a subcontractor insolvency mid-package is one of the most damaging events on a project, and the signals are usually visible months earlier. 4) Support package procurement: scope definition, tender list, and comparison of bids normalised for scope differences. 5) Manage payment applications: valuation against work actually completed, retention, and — critically — compliance with statutory payment terms and notice regimes, breach of which creates immediate liability. 6) Track performance: programme adherence, quality, safety and defect rates. 7) Manage the commercial relationship: variations, claims and final accounts. 8) Report supply-chain risk concentration across projects.
**Systems** — Subcontractor management, procurement, cost management, accounting, credit and compliance data.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Prequalification currency; subcontractor insolvency exposure; payment-notice compliance (100% required); package cost vs budget; performance scores.
**Human gate** — Payment certification is a contractual and personal responsibility; subcontractor appointment and termination are commercial decisions.

### RE-08 — Lease Abstraction & Portfolio Administration
**A2 · L3 · B3 · Record-to-report**
**Pain** — Lease terms live in PDFs. Rent reviews, breaks, options and obligations are missed, and portfolio-level exposure is unknown. Accounting standards (IFRS 16/ASC 842) require structured lease data that most portfolios do not have.
**Trigger** — Lease executed or acquired, portfolio review, or a lease event.
**Workflow** — 1) Extract the full term set from the lease and all amendments: parties, demise, term, rent and review mechanism, break rights and conditions, service charge, repair obligations, alienation, use restrictions, and options. 2) **Handle amendments and side letters** — the current position is the lease as varied, and this is the most common source of error in lease data. 3) Validate extraction with confidence scoring and route uncertain terms for human verification. 4) Build the event calendar: reviews, breaks, expiries, option exercise windows, and their conditions and notice requirements. Break conditions are frequently onerous and missing one forfeits the break. 5) Compute accounting treatment inputs for IFRS 16/ASC 842. 6) Monitor obligations: repair, insurance, compliance and reporting. 7) Analyse portfolio exposure: expiry profile, break optionality, rent to market, and covenant strength of tenants. 8) Alert with lead time proportionate to the decision's complexity.
**Systems** — Lease management system, document management, accounting/ERP, valuation data, portfolio analytics.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Abstraction accuracy; events missed (must be zero); lead time on decisions; IFRS 16 compliance; portfolio exposure visibility.
**Human gate** — Lease interpretation questions go to legal; all option exercises and notices are human-executed with legal review.

### RE-09 — Property Valuation & Investment Analysis
**A3 · L1 · B4 · Idea-to-market**
**Pain** — Valuation and investment appraisal require assembling comparables, market evidence, income analysis and assumptions; the work is manual and the evidence base is often thin.
**Trigger** — Valuation instruction, acquisition opportunity, or a portfolio review.
**Workflow** — 1) Assemble the property picture: physical, legal, tenancy, and planning position. 2) Gather comparable evidence: transactions, lettings and yields, assessed for genuine comparability rather than proximity — the discipline that separates a valuation from a number. 3) Analyse the income: passing rent, market rent, reversionary potential, void risk, tenant covenant strength, and lease structure. 4) Build the cash flow with explicit assumptions on rental growth, voids, capital expenditure, and exit yield. 5) **Run sensitivity and scenario analysis**, because the single-point valuation conceals the risk that matters. 6) Identify the value drivers and the risks specific to this asset: lease events, capital expenditure requirements, ESG compliance costs (increasingly the largest hidden liability), and planning constraints. 7) Draft the appraisal with the evidence, the assumptions and the reasoning. 8) For portfolios, aggregate and analyse concentration and exposure.
**Systems** — Valuation models, market data providers, lease data, planning data, EPC/ESG data.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Appraisal turnaround; comparable evidence quality; valuation variance vs subsequent transactions; assumptions documented; investment performance vs underwriting.
**Human gate** — **Formal valuations are signed by a qualified valuer** under professional standards (RICS Red Book or equivalent) with personal liability.

### RE-10 — Development Appraisal & Planning Support
**A3 · L1 · B4 · Idea-to-market**
**Pain** — Development appraisal involves cost, value, programme, planning risk and finance interacting over years. Planning applications are document-heavy and slow.
**Trigger** — Site opportunity, appraisal update, or a planning application milestone.
**Workflow** — 1) Assess the site: planning designation and policy, constraints (flood, heritage, ecology, contamination, access), and utilities capacity. 2) Test development scenarios: quantum, mix, and massing against policy and constraints. 3) Build the appraisal: gross development value from comparable evidence, construction cost from benchmarks, professional fees, finance, contingency, and the developer's return. 4) **Model planning risk explicitly** — probability, timescale, and the policy requirements (affordable housing, contributions, biodiversity net gain) that determine viability. 5) Run sensitivity on the variables that actually drive the outcome: sales values, build cost inflation, programme, and finance cost. 6) Support the planning application: document requirements, consultant coordination, and policy compliance mapping. 7) Track planning through determination with the consultation and committee position. 8) Monitor the appraisal through delivery against actuals.
**Systems** — Appraisal software (Argus), planning policy and constraint data, cost benchmarks, market data, GIS.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Appraisal turnaround; planning success rate; appraisal vs outturn variance; planning determination time; policy compliance.
**Human gate** — Development decisions are board-governed; planning strategy is professionally advised; viability assessments submitted to authorities are formally signed.

### RE-11 — Facilities Management & Building Performance
**A6 · L3 · B3 · Plan-to-produce**
**Pain** — Buildings are operated reactively; statutory compliance is tracked on spreadsheets; energy performance is poor and now carries regulatory and letting consequences.
**Trigger** — Fault, sensor anomaly, maintenance schedule, or a compliance deadline.
**Workflow** — 1) Manage statutory compliance: fire safety, legionella, electrical, lifts, gas, and asbestos — each with a defined frequency, competent-person requirement and record. **Non-compliance here is criminal, not commercial.** 2) Monitor building systems for faults, degradation and energy waste, distinguishing a control problem from a plant problem. 3) Triage and dispatch reactive work with diagnosis and priority. 4) Optimise planned maintenance against asset condition and criticality rather than a fixed calendar. 5) Analyse energy consumption against weather-normalised baselines and occupancy, and identify waste with a quantified saving. 6) Support ESG and regulatory performance requirements (EPC minimum standards, carbon reporting) with an improvement pathway and cost. 7) Manage contractor performance and compliance. 8) Maintain the asset register and lifecycle plan for capital forecasting.
**Systems** — CAFM, BMS, IoT sensors, compliance registers, contractor systems, energy monitoring.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Statutory compliance (100% required); reactive vs planned ratio; energy cost and intensity; asset availability; occupant satisfaction; lifecycle forecast accuracy.
**Human gate** — All statutory certification is by competent persons; safety-critical faults follow defined escalation.

### RE-12 — Construction Cost Control & Forecasting
**A6 · L2 · B4 · Record-to-report**
**Pain** — Project cost reporting lags reality by weeks; the cost to complete is estimated optimistically; and losses are recognised late, which is the classic construction failure pattern.
**Trigger** — Cost reporting cycle, commitment event, or a variance signal.
**Workflow** — 1) Consolidate the cost position: committed, incurred, accrued, and forecast, reconciled to the ledger rather than maintained in parallel. 2) Track actual production against measured work to derive genuine productivity, which is the honest basis for forecasting the remainder. 3) **Forecast cost to complete from evidence rather than from budget** — the difference between a forecast and a hope is whether it uses actual productivity. 4) Reconcile the cost position with the commercial position: are the variations claimed and recoverable, or is the cost incurred without entitlement? 5) Detect early warning signs: productivity decline, commitment overruns, unvalued variations, and accruals growing faster than progress. 6) Produce the cost report with variance explanations traced to cause. 7) Model the outturn under scenarios so management sees the range, not a point. 8) Support the cash-flow forecast, since construction insolvency is a cash event, not a profit event.
**Systems** — Cost management, ERP/accounting, procurement, planning, subcontractor systems.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Forecast accuracy at each stage; time to recognise variance; margin erosion detected early; cash-flow forecast accuracy; final account vs forecast.
**Human gate** — Commercial managers own forecasts; profit recognition and provisions are governed accounting judgements.

### RE-13 — ESG, Building Safety & Regulatory Compliance
**A8 · L2 · B4 · Risk-to-assurance**
**Pain** — Building safety regulation has tightened dramatically; ESG requirements (EPC minimums, embodied carbon, biodiversity net gain) are now determinative of lettability and value; evidence obligations are heavy and continuous.
**Trigger** — Regulatory requirement, asset review, project gateway, or a compliance deadline.
**Workflow** — 1) Maintain the compliance obligation register per asset and project, mapped to owners and evidence. 2) **Assemble and maintain the golden thread of building-safety information** for in-scope buildings: design intent, as-built information, materials, changes, and the accountable persons. This is a legal requirement most portfolios cannot currently satisfy. 3) Verify evidence completeness and detect gaps in historic records, prioritising by building risk. 4) Track fire-safety and structural obligations: assessments, remediation, and resident engagement requirements. 5) Manage ESG compliance: EPC ratings against minimum standards with the improvement pathway and cost, embodied and operational carbon reporting, and biodiversity obligations. 6) Assess the financial consequence of non-compliance: unlettable assets, remediation cost, and valuation impact. 7) Generate regulatory submissions and stakeholder reporting. 8) Monitor regulatory change and assess portfolio impact.
**Systems** — Asset information systems, document management, EPC and energy data, compliance registers, regulatory portals.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Golden-thread completeness for in-scope buildings; compliance gaps closed; assets meeting minimum standards; remediation programme progress; regulatory findings.
**Human gate** — **Accountable persons under building-safety legislation hold personal legal responsibility**; all safety cases and regulatory submissions are professionally signed.

## Regulatory notes for this vertical
| Topic | Impact on agent design |
|---|---|
| **Building safety regimes** | Golden-thread information obligations, accountable persons, and safety cases. Agent outputs become regulated records — retention, integrity and traceability matter. |
| **Statutory payment regimes** | Construction payment legislation imposes strict notice deadlines; a missed payless notice creates immediate liability. The agent's clock function is the highest-value control. |
| **Health & safety law** | Duties fall on named competent persons. Agents verify and prompt; they never authorise work. |
| **Professional standards (RICS etc.)** | Valuations and formal appraisals require qualified sign-off with personal liability. |
| **ESG disclosure & minimum standards** | Environmental claims and energy ratings are increasingly enforced; unevidenced claims are blocked. |
