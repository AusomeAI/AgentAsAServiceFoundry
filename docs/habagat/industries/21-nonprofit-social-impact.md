# Document 21 — Nonprofit, NGO & Social Impact

> 12 agent use cases. Notation per [Document 00](../00-research-method-and-taxonomy.md).

## Executive take
- Nonprofits have the **highest ratio of mission-critical work to available capacity** of any sector. Every hour an agent returns goes directly to beneficiaries, which makes the value proposition unusually clean.
- They also have the least money. Habagat should serve this sector through a **deliberately different commercial model** — a discounted tier, a shared-blueprint offering, or a foundation-funded programme. This is not charity: it is the sector where the reference stories are most powerful and where policy influence originates.
- Highest-value entry points: **NGO-02 Grant Writing & Funder Reporting** (the single largest administrative burden in the sector), **NGO-05 Beneficiary Intake & Referral**, **NGO-01 Donor Engagement**.
- Beneficiary data is among the most sensitive data in the catalogue — asylum status, domestic abuse, health, addiction. **Data protection here is a safety issue, not a compliance issue**, and design must reflect that.

### NGO-01 — Donor Engagement & Fundraising
**A7 · L2 · B3 · Demand creation**
**Pain** — Small fundraising teams manage large supporter bases; communications are generic; donor attrition is high and lapsed donors are rarely recovered.
**Trigger** — Donation, supporter interaction, lifecycle milestone, or a campaign.
**Workflow** — 1) Maintain the supporter picture: giving history, engagement, communication preferences, consent status, and the causes they actually care about. 2) Segment by value, engagement trajectory and motivation — **why someone gives determines what will keep them giving**, and most communications ignore this entirely. 3) Verify consent and preference before every contact, respecting frequency limits, since fundraising regulation is strict and reputational damage is severe. 4) Personalise around genuine relevance: the programme they supported, the outcome it produced, and the specific next need. 5) Report impact honestly and specifically — vague impact claims are the fastest route to donor cynicism. 6) Detect lapse risk from giving and engagement patterns and intervene before the lapse rather than after. 7) Manage the thanking and stewardship process promptly and specifically, which is the highest-return activity in fundraising and the most frequently neglected. 8) Support major-donor and trust relationships with research and briefings, within ethical fundraising boundaries. 9) Report on fundraising economics honestly, including the true cost of acquisition.
**Systems** — CRM/fundraising database, payment processing, email and campaign tools, consent management, programme data.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Donor retention; lifetime value; income per supporter; lapse recovery; **complaint and opt-out rate (guardrail)**; cost to raise a pound.
**Human gate** — Major-donor relationships are human; **vulnerable-donor protections apply — no pressure tactics, and any vulnerability indicator stops solicitation immediately**; ethical fundraising standards are enforced at the platform layer.

### NGO-02 — Grant Writing, Bid Development & Funder Reporting
**A3 · L1 · B3 · Order-to-cash**
**Pain** — Grant applications and reports are the single largest administrative burden in the sector. Small organisations lose the funding race to those who can afford bid writers, regardless of programme quality.
**Trigger** — Funding opportunity identified, or a report deadline.
**Workflow** — 1) Monitor funding opportunities and match them against the organisation's programmes, eligibility and capacity — including a realistic assessment of whether the grant's requirements are affordable to deliver. 2) Support the bid/no-bid decision honestly: fit, competition, effort, and whether the restricted funding would distort the mission, which is a real and under-discussed risk. 3) Assemble the evidence base: programme data, outcomes, beneficiary numbers, case studies with consent, and financial information. 4) Draft the application against the funder's specific questions, priorities and language, grounded in evidence rather than aspiration. 5) Build the budget against the funder's cost rules including full cost recovery where permitted — **under-recovering overheads is how nonprofits go insolvent while delivering successfully**. 6) Check compliance with every requirement before submission. 7) For reporting, assemble actuals against the agreed outcomes and budget, and explain variance honestly. 8) Maintain a library of approved content and evidence that compounds across applications. 9) Track the funding pipeline, restricted-fund position and reporting calendar.
**Systems** — Grant management, finance system, programme/outcome data, funder portals, document management.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Applications submitted and success rate; staff hours per application; full cost recovery achieved; reports submitted on time; content reuse.
**Human gate** — Submissions and financial declarations are approved by the accountable officer; programme commitments must be deliverable and are signed off by delivery leads.

### NGO-03 — Programme Monitoring, Evaluation & Learning
**A6 · L1 · B3 · Record-to-report**
**Pain** — Monitoring and evaluation is done because funders require it, not because it informs practice. Data is collected, reported and never used, which is a waste of both staff time and beneficiary goodwill.
**Trigger** — Data collection cycle, programme milestone, or an evaluation.
**Workflow** — 1) Structure data collection against the theory of change, and **collect only what will actually be used** — every unnecessary question costs frontline time and beneficiary patience. 2) Validate incoming data for completeness and plausibility, and support field teams with immediate feedback rather than corrections months later. 3) Analyse outputs and outcomes against targets, disaggregated by the groups that matter for equity. 4) Identify what is working and what is not, with honest treatment of uncertainty — a programme's evaluation should be capable of concluding that it did not work. 5) Detect delivery problems early enough to adapt, which is the entire practical point of monitoring. 6) Generate funder reports from the same evidence base rather than as a separate exercise. 7) Support learning: what should change in the programme, and what should be shared with the sector. 8) Distinguish attribution from contribution clearly, since overclaiming is endemic and corrosive.
**Systems** — M&E platform, mobile data collection (offline-capable), programme databases, finance, evaluation frameworks.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Data completeness and timeliness; programme adaptations driven by evidence; reporting effort; outcome achievement; learning shared.
**Human gate** — Evaluation conclusions are made by programme and evaluation leads; beneficiary data use requires consent and strict purpose limitation.

### NGO-04 — Volunteer Recruitment & Management
**A5 · L3 · B3 · Hire-to-retire**
**Pain** — Volunteers are the sector's largest resource and are managed with the least infrastructure: applications go unanswered, matching is poor, and volunteers drift away.
**Trigger** — Volunteer application, role need, or an engagement signal.
**Workflow** — 1) Process applications promptly — **the largest single cause of volunteer loss is a slow or absent response to an offer of help**. 2) Match volunteers to roles on skills, availability, location, motivation and accessibility needs. 3) Manage safeguarding requirements: background checks, references and training appropriate to the role's contact with vulnerable people. This is a hard gate that must be enforced before any placement. 4) Coordinate induction, training and placement. 5) Schedule against need, respecting availability and providing certainty, since volunteers are giving time and deserve reliable arrangements. 6) Recognise contribution meaningfully and specifically. 7) Detect disengagement early and understand why, rather than losing people silently. 8) Support volunteer wellbeing, particularly in emotionally demanding roles. 9) Report on volunteer contribution and value.
**Systems** — Volunteer management system, background-check providers, training platform, scheduling, communications.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Application response time; conversion to active volunteering; retention; safeguarding compliance (100% required); hours contributed; volunteer satisfaction.
**Human gate** — **Safeguarding decisions and role placement involving vulnerable people are human decisions**; background-check outcomes are assessed by designated staff.

### NGO-05 — Beneficiary Intake, Triage & Referral
**A1 · L2 · B4 · Issue-to-resolution**
**Pain** — People seeking help must navigate fragmented services and repeat their story many times. Waiting lists are long and the most urgent need is not reliably prioritised.
**Trigger** — Person contacts the service, or a referral is received.
**Workflow** — 1) Conduct intake with the person in mind, not the form: minimise repetition, allow disclosure at their pace, and avoid demanding traumatic detail that the service does not need. **Trauma-informed design is the requirement here, and it is a design constraint, not a tone.** 2) Identify immediate risk: safety, homelessness, suicidality, child protection, and domestic abuse — and **escalate immediately to a trained human on any risk indicator**, without completing the process. 3) Assess eligibility and need against the service's criteria and capacity. 4) Identify all relevant support, including services the person did not ask about and other organisations better placed to help. 5) Make warm referrals with consent, and provide the practical detail that makes a referral usable. 6) Manage waiting lists transparently, with prioritisation criteria that are stated and applied consistently. 7) Support the person while waiting rather than leaving them in silence. 8) Track outcomes and identify unmet need patterns for service design and advocacy. 9) Operate in the person's language and in accessible formats.
**Systems** — Case management, referral directories and networks, risk assessment frameworks, translation, communication channels.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Time to first response; risk identified and escalated appropriately; referral completion rate; waiting-list transparency; unmet need documented; beneficiary feedback.
**Human gate** — **Every risk indicator routes to a trained human immediately.** Eligibility and prioritisation decisions are human. The agent never assesses safeguarding risk as a conclusion.

### NGO-06 — Case Management & Support Coordination
**A5 · L2 · B4 · Issue-to-resolution**
**Pain** — Support workers carry high caseloads with heavy recording requirements; administration displaces the direct support that is the whole point of the role.
**Trigger** — Case activity, review due, or a support need.
**Workflow** — 1) Maintain the case record with support plans, actions, and progress against goals set with the person rather than for them. 2) **Reduce recording burden**: structure notes from the worker's own account, prompt for what is genuinely required, and never require duplicate entry across systems. This is the sector's most-requested improvement. 3) Track actions and deadlines across the multiple agencies typically involved, and chase what has not happened. 4) Support advocacy: benefits entitlement, housing applications, appeals, and rights information, with accurate current guidance. 5) Prompt reviews and identify cases where progress has stalled or risk has changed. 6) Surface relevant history and patterns across the record that a worker with a high caseload cannot hold in mind. 7) Support handover and continuity, which is where vulnerable people most often fall through gaps. 8) Aggregate anonymised case data for service planning and advocacy.
**Systems** — Case management, benefits and entitlement information, partner agency systems, document management.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Worker time on direct support vs administration; case progression; entitlements secured; cases stalled; continuity through handover; outcomes achieved.
**Human gate** — **All support decisions and all risk judgements are the worker's.** Safeguarding follows statutory process. Beneficiary data is highly restricted and access is audited.

### NGO-07 — Humanitarian Response & Field Operations
**A5 · L2 · B4 · Plan-to-produce**
**Pain** — Emergency response requires rapid needs assessment, resource mobilisation and coordination across agencies, in environments with poor connectivity and severe access constraints.
**Trigger** — Emergency onset, situation change, or an assessment cycle.
**Workflow** — 1) Assemble the situational picture from assessments, partner reports, satellite and open-source data, and government information, with source reliability graded. 2) Estimate affected populations and needs by sector and location, with the uncertainty stated — false precision in humanitarian assessment drives bad resource allocation. 3) Support coordination: who is doing what where, gaps, and duplication across the response. 4) Plan the response against access constraints, security, logistics capacity and funding. 5) Manage the supply pipeline: procurement, transport, customs clearance, warehousing and distribution, in environments where each of these fails routinely. 6) Support beneficiary registration and assistance delivery with **strict data protection, because in conflict and displacement contexts beneficiary data can endanger lives** — this is the most acute data-safety requirement in the entire catalogue. 7) Monitor delivery and detect diversion or exclusion. 8) Support reporting to donors and coordination bodies.
**Systems** — Humanitarian information systems, logistics and supply chain, beneficiary registration, coordination platforms, satellite and geospatial data.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Time from onset to first delivery; population reached; coverage against assessed need; pipeline reliability; data-protection incidents (must be zero); duplication avoided.
**Human gate** — **All operational and access decisions are human and security-governed.** Beneficiary data handling follows humanitarian data protection standards with the strictest possible controls; do-no-harm assessment precedes all data collection.

### NGO-08 — Advocacy, Policy & Campaigning
**A3 · L1 · B3 · Demand creation**
**Pain** — Small policy teams must track legislation, build evidence and mobilise supporters against far better-resourced opposition.
**Trigger** — Policy development, consultation, legislative stage, or a campaign moment.
**Workflow** — 1) Monitor legislative and policy developments relevant to the mission across the relevant jurisdictions and levels of government. 2) Assess impact on the people the organisation serves, grounded in its own case and programme evidence — **the sector's distinctive asset is frontline evidence, and it is chronically under-used in policy work**. 3) Build the evidence base: internal data, external research, and case studies with informed consent and appropriate anonymisation. 4) Draft consultation responses, briefings and position papers tailored to the audience — a select committee briefing and a supporter email are different documents. 5) Support parliamentary and stakeholder engagement with the relevant positions, voting records and prior statements. 6) Manage campaign mobilisation with supporter communications and action tools, respecting consent. 7) Track policy outcomes and the organisation's contribution honestly. 8) Ensure compliance with lobbying transparency and, where applicable, charity political-activity rules.
**Systems** — Policy monitoring, parliamentary data, CRM/supporter database, campaign tools, research repository.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Policy developments tracked and responded to; briefings produced; engagement with decision-makers; policy changes influenced; supporter mobilisation; compliance with charity campaigning rules.
**Human gate** — Policy positions are approved by leadership and trustees; **charity law restricts political activity and this is a hard compliance boundary**; case studies require informed, revocable consent.

### NGO-09 — Finance, Restricted Funds & Charity Compliance
**A8 · L2 · B4 · Record-to-report**
**Pain** — Charity finance is unusually complex: restricted and unrestricted funds, grant conditions, and specific reporting standards, managed by small finance teams under regulator scrutiny.
**Trigger** — Transaction, reporting period, grant condition, or an audit.
**Workflow** — 1) Ensure expenditure is coded to the correct fund and that **restricted funds are spent only on their restricted purpose** — a breach is a serious regulatory matter and it happens through coding error, not intent. 2) Monitor fund balances, spend against grant conditions and timelines, and flag underspend risk early enough to request a variation. 3) Support full cost recovery: allocate overheads correctly across funds and grants per the applicable rules. 4) Manage the reporting calendar across funders, regulators and statutory requirements. 5) Prepare statutory accounts to the applicable charity standard, including the reserves policy and fund disclosures. 6) Support the trustees' report with programme and financial evidence. 7) Monitor the financial health indicators the sector's regulator and funders watch: reserves, going concern, and dependency concentration. 8) Support audit and independent examination with the evidence assembled.
**Systems** — Finance/accounting system, grant management, payroll, banking, reporting tools.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Restricted-fund breaches (must be zero); reporting timeliness; full cost recovery achieved; audit findings; reserves against policy; underspend forfeited.
**Human gate** — Trustees and the accountable officer approve accounts and financial decisions; regulatory filings are legally attributable.

### NGO-10 — Safeguarding, Risk & Organisational Assurance
**A8 · L2 · B4 · Risk-to-assurance**
**Pain** — Safeguarding failures have destroyed major organisations. Small charities have limited assurance capacity, and risks are often visible in fragments across the organisation.
**Trigger** — Concern raised, incident, risk review, or a compliance cycle.
**Workflow** — 1) Capture concerns and incidents with structured, contemporaneous detail. 2) **Escalate immediately to the designated safeguarding lead** for anything meeting the threshold, with no queue and no triage delay. 3) Assemble relevant history with strict access control, surfacing patterns that individuals cannot see. 4) Prompt the required process: statutory referrals, regulator serious-incident reporting, and internal process, each with its own deadline. 5) Manage the risk register with owners, controls and review dates, and test whether controls are actually operating. 6) Monitor compliance obligations: safeguarding policies, training currency, background checks, data protection, and health and safety. 7) Track actions to verified closure. 8) Support trustee assurance reporting with an honest picture, including what is not working.
**Systems** — Case management, HR and training records, risk register, incident reporting, regulator portals.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Time from concern to designated lead; statutory and regulator reporting timeliness (100% required); training and check currency; action closure; assurance coverage.
**Human gate** — **All safeguarding decisions are made by the designated lead**; serious incident reporting is made by trustees or the accountable officer; the agent surfaces, structures and prompts only.

### NGO-11 — Community Engagement & Service Design
**A3 · L1 · B3 · Idea-to-market**
**Pain** — Services are designed for communities rather than with them; feedback is collected sporadically and rarely changes anything, which erodes trust.
**Trigger** — Service design cycle, feedback received, or a community consultation.
**Workflow** — 1) Aggregate feedback across channels: surveys, complaints, frontline observations, community meetings and informal channels. 2) Analyse for themes with **explicit attention to whose voice is missing** — feedback systems systematically over-represent the already-engaged, and designing from that data entrenches exclusion. 3) Identify barriers to access: language, digital exclusion, transport, timing, trust, and eligibility complexity. 4) Support co-design with communities by structuring, summarising and returning what was heard accurately. 5) Analyse service data for equity: who accesses, who completes, who benefits, disaggregated by the characteristics that matter. 6) Model service changes and their likely effect on access and outcomes. 7) **Close the loop**: report back to the community on what was heard and what changed, which is the step that determines whether anyone participates next time. 8) Track whether service changes improved access and outcomes.
**Systems** — Feedback and survey tools, case management, community engagement records, service data.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Feedback representativeness; barriers identified and removed; equity of access and outcomes; changes made from feedback; community trust measures.
**Human gate** — Service design decisions involve the community and leadership; the agent never speaks for a community.

### NGO-12 — Operations, HR & Shared Services
**A5 · L3 · B2 · Hire-to-retire**
**Pain** — Small back-office teams cover HR, IT, facilities and procurement for the whole organisation; administrative load displaces mission delivery.
**Trigger** — Staff request, operational event, or an administrative cycle.
**Workflow** — 1) Handle routine staff enquiries: policy, leave, pay, benefits, and process, with correct and current answers. 2) Orchestrate onboarding and offboarding across HR, IT, access and payroll (see UC-X09). 3) Manage recruitment administration with fair-process compliance and prompt candidate communication, since slow recruitment is costly in a sector with tight labour markets. 4) Track mandatory training, checks and policy attestations with renewal management. 5) Support procurement within the organisation's rules and grant conditions, which frequently impose specific requirements. 6) Manage IT and facilities requests with triage and dispatch. 7) Support wellbeing: monitor workload and leave patterns at a team level to identify burnout risk, which is acute in frontline roles and rarely surfaced until someone leaves. 8) Report on operational performance and cost.
**Systems** — HR system, payroll, IT service management, finance/procurement, training platform.
**Outcome & KPIs** — Query resolution time; onboarding readiness; training compliance; recruitment cycle time; staff turnover; admin cost as % of expenditure.
**Human gate** — All employment decisions are human; wellbeing analysis is team-level only and never used for individual performance management.

## Ethical & regulatory notes for this vertical
| Topic | Impact on agent design |
|---|---|
| **Beneficiary data** | This is the most sensitive data class in the catalogue. In humanitarian and asylum contexts, a data breach can endanger lives. Design to the strictest standard: minimum collection, strong access control, short retention, and do-no-harm assessment before any collection. |
| **Safeguarding** | Any indicator routes immediately to a designated human lead. The agent never assesses risk to a person as a conclusion. |
| **Trauma-informed design** | Intake and interaction design must avoid re-traumatisation: no unnecessary repetition, no demand for detail the service does not need, and always a route to a person. |
| **Charity regulation** | Political activity restrictions, restricted-fund rules, trustee accountability and serious-incident reporting are hard legal boundaries. |
| **Fundraising standards** | Vulnerable-donor protections, consent, frequency limits, and prohibitions on pressure tactics are enforced at the platform layer. |
| **Digital exclusion** | An agent-first service excludes the people most in need. Every agent-mediated service must have an equally accessible human route, and this should be a contractual commitment. |
