Doc 41The thesis

Four reasons agents stall before production — all of them operating problems

Enterprises have not been short of AI enthusiasm. They have been short of AI in production. The reasons repeat across every enterprise in this study, and none of them is a modelling problem.

Nobody can prove it's right

No evaluation corpus, so no basis for trusting an agent to act unattended. Confidence is asserted, not measured.

Nobody will accept the data risk

Multi-tenant AI vendors fail the security review in banking, healthcare and government — often before the demo.

Nobody owns the operation

A pilot ships. Then it needs monitoring, improvement, incident response and governance. No one is resourced for it.

Regulation arrived first

The EU AI Act and sector rules demand documentation and oversight a demonstration cannot satisfy.

The answer to “isn't this just a wrapper?” A wrapper calls a model. Habagat computes an authorisation envelope before the model sees untrusted content, enforces deterministic rules the model cannot override, compensates partial multi-system writes, produces a regulator-grade trace of every action, and gates every autonomy increase on a versioned evaluation corpus. Swap the model out — the provider-neutral spec allows it — and the product still exists. That is the test.

Doc 00 · 33Governance model

Autonomy is earned, never assumed

Every agent ships at L1 or L2 and is promoted only on evidence. The level an agent operates at — paired with its blast radius — determines its engineering cost, its governance regime and its gross margin.

The promotion gate from L2 → L3

Evaluation threshold met on the customer's own data · ≥14 days shadow operation with disagreements analysed individually · false-action rate below SLO at a lower confidence bound · escalations shown to be genuinely necessary · every automated write has a tested compensating action · review-queue capacity confirmed · the customer's AI Council decision documented with a named owner · a rollback to L2 rehearsed and timed at under one hour. Demotion on breach is automatic and requires no meeting.

Doc 30Reference architecture

Two planes, and a boundary that is continuously tested

A single Habagat control plane provisions, versions, observes and bills. N fully isolated customer data planes execute. Customer content never enters the control plane — and a scheduled canary plants unique markers in tenant data and searches the control plane for them. Any hit is a P1 incident.

What isolation costs

A fixed floor of roughly $1,100–2,200 per tenant per month before a single token is spent, and N deployments to operate rather than one. This is stated openly in the pricing model rather than discovered later.

What isolation buys

The ability to sell to a bank, a hospital and a government in the same quarter. Data residency by construction. A breach blast radius of one customer. And a genuine exit — it is their tenant.

Docs 40 · CTO 04Unit economics

The margin lever is autonomy, not tokens

In the worked cost model, inference is a minority of COGS and human review is the largest single line. That single fact should shape the roadmap, the delivery incentives and the investor narrative — and it makes the operating metric unusually clear.

Year-one gross margin on a new customer is about 48%. It reaches 65–72% as agents are promoted from L2 to L3 and review load falls away. So the number the company is managed by — and the number a board should hold it to — is the L3 promotion rate across the fleet. If autonomy stalls at L2, Habagat is a well-run services business rather than a software company. Every roadmap decision is therefore biased toward producing the evidence that unlocks L3.

Doc 36 · 41Defensibility

Four assets, none of them the model

The Evaluation Corpus the moat

Graded cases per blueprint across five bands — happy path, common variation, edge, adversarial, and should-escalate. Grown from human corrections and production incidents across every customer. It cannot be bought or synthesised: it requires production deployments across many enterprises over time. The corpus for blueprint one costs five person-weeks; for customer twelve of that blueprint, three days.

Vertical Blueprints

284 workflows mapped to eight archetypes, with domain grounding, KPIs and regulatory constraints encoded. Requires domain expertise plus deployment experience — the combination a generalist platform does not accumulate.

The Isolation Fabric

The automation that provisions and operates hundreds of independent tenants without them becoming snowflakes: declarative specs, drift detection, ring rollout, signed bundles that fail to load if modified. Unglamorous, and the reason single-tenant can be sold profitably.

The Trust Layer

Policy envelopes computed before untrusted content is read, deterministic verification, compensating actions, audit-grade tracing, automatic model cards. The part regulated buyers require and most vendors have not built — and the part that gets a vendor past second-line risk review.

Why the should-escalate band matters most. Roughly a tenth of every evaluation corpus consists of cases where the correct behaviour is not to act. It measures whether the agent knows its own limits — the property the entire autonomy model rests on. Ranked by importance the metrics run: escalation recall, then hard-rule adherence, then calibration, then task success. Most teams reverse that order, which is why their agents demo well and fail in production.
